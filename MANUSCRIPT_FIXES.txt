1. §V Exp. 2 — Scheme [41]'s upper points are EXTRAPOLATED, not measured
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE   §V, Experiment 2, and the caption of fig:exp2.

WHY     Scheme [41] (Thingom) is measured only at N=10^4. The 50k / 100k /
        500k / 1M points are produced by scaling that anchor linearly
        (infra/extrapolate_points.py); they carry n_runs=1 and a blank CI, and
        generate_plots.py draws them as HOLLOW markers. A hollow marker tells
        a reader something differs, not what.

        Its search is O(N) pairings with no early termination, as published.
        One run at N=10^6 is ~20.5 h extrapolated from its own measured 10^4
        anchor (737.7 s/run), so 10 runs + 5 warm-ups is ~307 h for that point
        alone.

ADD     to the fig:exp2 caption:
        "Scheme~\cite{ref41}'s points above $N=10^4$ are extrapolated linearly
        from its measured $N=10^4$ anchor and are drawn with hollow markers;
        its search is $O(N)$ pairings with no early termination, which places
        a full sweep beyond the per-track compute budget."

STATUS  A measurement campaign for these points is running; if it completes,
        delete this item and the hollow markers with it.

3. §V Exp. 8 — least_loaded wins the spread metric, and that is fine
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE   §V, Experiment 8.

WHY     `least_loaded` takes the lowest utilization standard deviation
        (0.0437 against AASS's 0.0587 at concurrency 10000). It minimises
        queue length by construction, so it wins that metric by construction.
        AASS trades spread for authorization locality. Saying AASS wins every
        metric invites a reviewer to check, and the check fails.

ADD     "Least-loaded scheduling attains the lowest utilization spread, which
        follows from its rule; AASS trades a small amount of spread for
        authorization locality and leads on throughput at every concurrency."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. §V Exp. 6 — Full-State's cost is a LOWER BOUND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE   §V, Experiment 6.

WHY     The Full-State arm redistributes authorization state to every FSN, but
        models each authority's commitment RECOMPUTATION as a digest rather
        than a Merkle rebuild. The benchmark world holds authority commitments
        as fixed values with no state behind them, and giving them one means
        choosing a state size the paper does not publish.

ADD     "The Full-State arm's authorization-state reconstruction is modelled
        by its commitment digest rather than a full re-derivation, so its
        reported cost is a lower bound."

5. §V Exp. 6 — the selective saving is in DELIVERY VOLUME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE   §V, Experiment 6, and the fig:exp6 caption.

WHY     DIAS and Incremental-All evolve identical policy sets and differ only
        in fan-out. In an in-process harness a delivery is a function call, so
        the selective saving shows in bytes (a clean 4x) and only 2-11% in
        latency. A reader given only the latency panel sees two curves nearly
        on top of each other.

ADD     "Because the harness models no network, Incremental-All's unnecessary
        propagation appears chiefly in transferred data rather than in
        sender-side latency."
